=> Convention note: the published distribution is the NODE DEGREE
   DISTRIBUTION IN EDGE VIEW, i.e. the edge-perspective variable-degree
   polynomial lambda(x) = sum_i lambda_i x^{i-1} (paper eq. 13).  The
   printed exponents {1,3,6,9,18,25,27} therefore correspond to variable
   DEGREES {2,4,7,10,19,26,28}.

MC-DE CONVENTIONS (verbatim)
----------------------------
"We used 100000 nodes with a maximum of 150 iterations for the MC-DE, the
 QBER was swept in 20 steps in a short range below the best possible
 threshold."
Entropy-based convergence: "During the simulation, we track the average
 entropy of all messages. When it falls below a certain value, decoding is
 considered successful." (Section 2.2.2)
Concentrated check distribution: "Utilizing a concentrated check node
 distribution (which is favorable according to [44]) and a fixed code
 rate, we can further simplify to E(lambda)." (Section 2.2.2)
=> Check distribution inference (documented, not verbatim): the two-point
   concentrated check-degree distribution is implied by the fixed rate,
   dc_mean = 1 / ((1 - R) * integral_lambda) with
   integral_lambda = sum_i lambda_i / degree_i over the effective degrees
   {2,4,7,10,19,26,28} = 0.16441561596298437, giving
   dc_mean = 24.328589328768736 and the two-point distribution
   {24: 0.6714106712, 25: 0.3285893288}.
